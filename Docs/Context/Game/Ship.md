# The ship

> Part of the Game documentation ([`Docs/Context/Game/CONTEXT.md`](CONTEXT.md)) — read that first.

The players' FLYING MAGIC SHIP (`GameShip`, `Code/Game/Private/Ship.ixx` / `Ship.cpp`): the sandbox world only, the
authority only. 56 x 22 m - 28 x 11 grid tiles, blocky. **THE SHIP IS THE BASE**: the Beacon stands on its deck, the
players build on the deck, and it LANDS on the world's grid, where its deck joins the base (road, buildings, attacks).
Flying, everything on it is safe and goes with it. The plan for it: transport (the players, goods and units between
bases), combat support and progression; cargo, combat and multiplayer sync are not there yet.

## The pieces

| Piece | What |
|---|---|
| The look | `Entities/Game/airship.pre`: RENDER ONLY, `Global true`, `CullMode RootOnly`; the hull is the root, the deck planks (`ShipDeck` / `ShipDeckB`: one plate per 2 m tile column, two alternating tones, so the tile grid reads on the deck), and the trim (`ShipTrim`: a dark wale along the hull's sides, a bowsprit 6 m out over the bow, a rudder: a post against the stern and its blade out behind it LOW, under the stern entrance's stairs, top at y -2.3) are children. No wheel: the pilot stands behind the Beacon. THE MAGIC: eight light crystals (`gemsGlow`'s `Diamond`, unlit with an `Emissive`, so the bloom takes them) half in the hull - out of the second tier's sides, out of the stem and a large one under the keel. `Models/airship.obj`: BLOCKY, whole 2 m grid tiles - 28 x 11 tiles (56 x 22 m, about 2.5 : 1; the bow z -28, the stern +28). The outline is a half-width in tiles per 2 m row (`c_rows` in `Ship.cpp`, `GameShip::rowHalfTiles`: 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, then 5 x 12, then 4 x 6, then 3 x 2 - a bow that narrows a tile every two rows, the widest beam over the middle (z -12 .. 12), a wide rear and a short flat 7-tile stern; the plan view of a common Minecraft ship schematic, with a broader stern) around a CENTRE COLUMN: a row of half-width h holds the tiles -h .. h (2h + 1), so the widest row is 11 tiles and the Beacon (3 x 3) stands on the middle line. The ORIGIN stays on a tile corner (the landing grid needs it), so the middle line is x = 1 (`GameShip::DeckCentreX`) and the beam x -10 .. 12; the keel, the bowsprit, the rudder and the collider are centred on it. Tried 24 x 12 tiles with straight sides, dropped because from above it read as a box, not a ship. Under the 2 m deck tier two tiers step in by a tile each side and start further back at the bow than at the stern (the stem RAKES forward), then a keel. The origin is the DECK TOP's centre, the bow points -Z, the keel's bottom is y -7 (`GameShip::KeelDepth`). It is GENERATED from that row table (a one-off script, not in the repo): edit the OBJ by hand or regenerate, and keep `c_rows` the same outline (a `static_assert` checks its length against `DeckHalfLength`). **The wall is not in the model**: it is the rail structures (below). |
| The collider | A KINEMATIC body `GameShip::makeBody` creates (not the prefab's: a prefab has one shape and the Physics component never moves a kinematic body), BUILT FROM `c_rows`: per tier one box per run of equal half-width (the deck tier, then the two lower tiers shrunk and trimmed like the model's), plus the keel (Default layer), plus - WHILE IT FLIES - a box per structure on the deck (`setStructureBoxes` from `StructureSystem::shipColliderBoxes`: the footprint x the type's height; none for rubble; none while landed, when the structures have their own colliders). A change of those boxes (`StructureSystem::shipRevision`) rebuilds the body in `tickShip`. Players and units stand on it and are pushed by it. It does not collide with the terrain or the buildings (kinematic). |
| The pose | `GameShip` integrates its OWN state (`m_pos`, `m_rot`, the velocities) and writes the entity's `pos` / `rot` from it. The body gets the state's OWN velocity (and the yaw rate as its spin) plus a gentle pull onto the pose (4/s, at most 4 m/s; more than 8 m off: a teleport; `driveBody`). Tried a velocity that closes the whole gap in one frame, dropped because a physics step longer than a frame overshoots by steps/frames each time: the collider swung ever faster (the look showed nothing) and launched a player who touched it kilometres up. |
| When | `GameMatch::updatePlayer` → `tickShip`, main thread, PRE-PHYSICS, before the player's own movement (its walk is relative to the deck): the flight, then `StructureSystem::setShipPose` + `tickShipStructures` (the structures on the deck follow; the landing / take-off edges), the body's boxes, and while landed `m_basePos` = the Beacon's ground. |
| The start | `tickBaseSite`: the game starts IN THE AIR - the ship FLIES 50 m over the start (the deck top over the ground at the anchor; its middle line over the anchor, the bow along the start view; the wanted keel height `setWantedHeight(50 - KeelDepth)`, so it hovers there), the Beacon on its deck (`spawnShipBase`) and the rails on its edge (`ensureShipRails`), all aloft. The local player spawns AT THE HELM, piloting: the first thing to do is pick a landing site (L). Its respawn point stays on the ground at the anchor, and `m_basePos` is that ground until the ship lands. No home site yet. "Game/Ship" "Enabled" off = no ship, and the Beacon stands on the land as before. |
| Save | `ShipPos`, `ShipYaw`, and for a landed (or landing) ship `ShipLanded` + `ShipTurns`, and the home site `ShipHome` + `ShipHomeTurns` (F9). A load puts the ship there FIRST (landed: `landAt`; else at rest), so the structures on its deck load onto it, then `ensureShipRails` mends a wall the save lacks. |

## The flight (`GameShip::tick`, "Game/Ship" tweaks)

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Throttle | A LEVER, not a pedal: W raises it toward full, S lowers it toward reverse (-0.35), released it HOLDS ("Throttle rate (/s)" 0.8). Nobody at the helm: it eases back to stop. **X BRAKES** (`Input::brake`): the lever goes to stop at 4x the rate and the hull loses "Brake (m/s2)" (5) of its forward speed per second until it stands (never past zero). The thrust ("Thrust (m/s2)" 3, reverse a third) pushes along the bow against a QUADRATIC drag sized so they meet at "Max speed (m/s)" (30), plus a little linear drag. |
| Keel | The sideways slip dies at "Side drag (/s)" (1.2), and the velocity turns with the hull. |
| Rudder | A / D: the spin rate goes toward "Turn rate (deg/s)" (21.5) x (0.35 + 0.65 x the speed share) at "Turn accel (deg/s2)" (14.5), and dies at "Turn drag (/s)" with no rudder - the hull's weight. |
| Height | Space / Ctrl move the WANTED height of the keel over the ground at "Climb rate (m/s)" (4), between "Min height (m)" (1) and "Max height (m)" (80, the limit). The ground is the HIGHEST of 7 samples under the hull (`groundY`: the terrain layer) and 2 ahead along the velocity ("Look ahead (s)" 2.5) - so it climbs before a hill. Water that is not deep counts as a surface. A spring-damper ("Vertical stiffness" 0.9, "Vertical damping" 1.4, "Max vertical accel (m/s2)" 3) moves it there: slow to start, slow to stop. Under the FLOOR (the keel 0.5 m over the surface under it; afloat 1.5 m under the float height) it is PUSHED up (3x the depth per second, at most 12 m/s), never moved: a snap was a teleport. |
| A wall | Ground ahead more than 6 m over the keel (it cannot climb in time) brakes it. |
| THE SEA | Under the deck's centre, water over a seabed more than "Deep water (m)" (8) under the sea level is THE SEA - judged by the BED, never by the surface, which the waves move by metres (a crest far over the sea level was "no sea" and threw it into the air). A river's or a lake's bed lies over the sea level. It sails at once over the sea and stops only after 1 s off it (`m_seaLeave`). The magic does not hold there. It SAILS: it FLOATS on the water with the deck "Freeboard (m)" (2.5: 4.5 m of the 7 m hull under water) over the mean surface, the height keys do nothing, the top speed is x "Sea speed (x)" (1: as fast as in the air). Afloat the vertical spring is "Sea stiffness" (2.5) / "Sea damping" (2.2) - the water carries the hull firmly. Rivers and lakes (shallow, or high over the sea level) do not count, and a deep corner does not lift it. Its bow over DRY land (a sample whose ground is over the water) lifts it out of the water over the land; the water itself never lifts it. |
| THE WAVES | Afloat, the water height (`PhysicsWorld::sampleWaterHeight`: the ocean's displaced surface) at the BOW (24 m ahead), the STERN (24 m back) and BOTH SIDES (10 m; all from `DeckHalfLength` / `DeckHalfWidth`) gives the float height (their mean with the centre's), the PITCH (`atan2(bow - stern, 48)`) and the ROLL (`atan2(right - left, 20)`), x "Wave follow (x)" (1). No body per corner: four samples a tick. |
| The look | It BANKS into a turn ("Bank per turn" 1.5 x the turn rate x the speed share, up to "Max bank (deg)" 30), PITCHES with the forward acceleration (and in the air the vertical one; up to "Max pitch (deg)" 3) and in the air BOBS ("Bob height (m)" 0.25 over "Bob period (s)" 6). At sea the waves give the bob, the pitch and the roll. Smoothed over ~0.5 s (~0.33 s afloat). The deck tilts too: it is the body's pose, so a standing player rides it. |

## Landing (L at the helm)

A LANDED ship sits ON THE WORLD'S 2 m GRID: its yaw a quarter turn, its centre on a tile corner (x, z multiples of 2 m:
the origin is a tile corner, whatever the deck's width), so **every deck tile is a world grid cell**. Its deck top is at the
ground. Then it is part of the base: its structures join the world, its free tiles are road, and it can be attacked.

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| L | At the helm (`updateModeSwitching` → `toggleShipLanding`): flying, it starts (or cancels) the LANDING PLACEMENT; grounded (landed, or on its way there), it takes off. |
| The placement | `updateLandPlacement` (it takes the clicks in `updateGameInput`: no beam, no build, no move order): like a building's ghost. The crosshair's ray on the TERRAIN (the ship's own deck never stops it, up to 400 m) puts the deck's MIDDLE LINE there; the centre snaps to a tile corner; Q / E turn it a quarter. `drawShipFootprint` draws the deck's outline 0.3 m over the site and the bow's line: green where it can land, red where not (the HUD counter "Ship" says why, or "too far" past 300 m from the ship), blue at the home site. **A good site farther than "Land distance (m)" (20: 10 tiles) from the ship is YELLOW** ("fly closer: N m"): it shows where the ship could land, but LMB does nothing there - fly over it first, so the landing's travel is no fast way to move the ship (the home site too). LMB: `land()` there. RMB or L: cancel; letting go of the helm too. The site test (`shipLandingSite`) runs only when the snapped place or the turn changes. It works while sailing too (the site is on land). |
| THE HOME SITE | Where the ship last stood landed (`m_shipHome` / `m_shipHomeTurns`, set every tick while landed; saved as `ShipHome` / `ShipHomeTurns`). The placement starts in the home's turn, and within 8 m of the home (in that turn) the ghost SNAPS to it: take off, fly, and land on the very same tiles again. |
| The site | `GameMatch::shipLandingSite(at, turns)`: the centre snapped (`GameShip::snapCentre`) in the placement's quarter turn. One ground ray per deck tile (the collider: `StructureSystem::groundHeightAt`): the deck top goes to the HIGHEST ground under it (+5 cm: no ground pokes through), the rest of the hull in the ground. REFUSED (red ghost; the HUD counter says why): no ground under a tile, water over the ground at a tile, a ground step under the deck over `KeelDepth` - 1 (6 m: the hull would hang in the air at the low side), a tree or rock (the Vegetation layer) through the hull (one box per run of rows, from the lowest ground to 3 m over the deck), a land structure on a deck cell (`StructureSystem::shipLandingClear`). |
| The settle | `land()`: the flight is off (`tickGrounded`). FAR (more than 2 m across): it TRAVELS over at the cruise height (`m_travelY`: its height at the click, at least 12 m over the site, and raised to clear the ground under it and 25 m ahead by `KeelDepth` + "Min height (m)" (at least 2) + 2), at up to 20 m/s (1 + 0.6 x the distance for the last stretch), climbing at most 6 m/s and sinking 4 m/s, the yaw turning to the site's on the way (at most 0.5 rad/s). Then the SET-DOWN: the pose closes the gap at 1.5/s (at most 10 m/s, at least 1.5 m/s; the yaw at least 0.15 rad/s) and the pitch and bank go level; within 3 cm and 0.002 rad it is LANDED, exactly on the pose. Tried a pure 1.5/s approach, dropped because its tail took seconds, and until LANDED the deck's buildings are out of the world ("No road", no workers). At the join / leave edge the deck's buildings re-check their badges at once and the walkers' ~1 s pass runs at once (as it also does when any structure is placed). Landed, it holds there (velocity 0) whoever is at the helm. It passes through what is in the way (kinematic). |
| Take off | `takeOff()`: the flight takes over at rest with the wanted height it had before it landed (at least "Min height (m)" + 2); the hull is in the ground, so the floor push lifts it out. The floor push is never faster than the hover can brake before the wanted height (`sqrt(2 x 0.8 x max accel x gap)`), so it does not fly past it and sink back. |
| THE STAIRS | At the landing edge `StructureSystem::buildGangways` lays a flight of stairs out of every ENTRANCE (as wide as it: one 2 m step piece per tile, turned to the entrance's side), 0.5 m out and 0.375 m down a step (37 degrees; tried 45 degrees, dropped because bodies climbed it badly), until a step's top meets the ground under it (the highest of three rays across the width), at most 28 steps; a deck only a little over the ground (more than 5 cm) still gets ONE step, its top no lower than 5 cm over the ground; a deck at the ground or under it gets none, and an entrance whose cells out there hold a land structure gets none. The steps are render-only `gangwayStep.pre` (`Models/shipRail.obj`'s `GangwayStep`; 1 m tall, so the steps overlap); the bodies walk on ONE smooth RAMP, a sloped static box at the same slope: flush with the deck at its edge (no lip, nothing over the deck) and running on into the ground at the foot (to 0.3 m under it, at most 3 m past the last step), so neither end is a lip. It lies over the step tops by at most one rise. Their cells are ROAD (`m_gangwayRoad`: `roadAt` gives the ramp's height at the cell centre), so a road built up to the foot of the stairs joins the deck, and a land placement there is refused. The take-off removes them. **A probe that finds NO terrain collider** (it is not built there yet: a load right after the start) sets `m_gangwayRetry`, and `tickShipStructures` builds all the stairs again ~30 ticks later, until every probe finds ground. |
| The start, a load | `landAt()`: landed at once, no settle. |

## Building on the ship (`StructureSystem`, `StructuresShip.cpp`)

A structure ON THE SHIP (`Ref::onShip`) keeps its place on the deck in SHIP SPACE (`Ref::shipLocal`: its ground point,
y 0 = the deck top; x, z on the deck's tiles by `snapToGrid`'s rule). Every tick `tickShipStructures` puts its entity
(and a rubble pile) on the ship's pose.

| State | What a ship structure is |
|---|---|
| FLYING (aloft) | OUT OF THE WORLD (`shipLeave`): no grid cells, its own collider off (`SetEnabled` 0; the ship's body carries its box), `invulnerable` (no unit bites it, no shot or field hurts it, the waves do not come for it, no Nav obstacle - `gatherNavFeed` skips invulnerable structures). It still works: production, stores, its field (a `ForceComponent` follows its entity), the light links (derived from positions about once a second, so a hub on the ship links to one on the land in range, and the link breaks when it flies off). |
| LANDED | IN THE WORLD (`shipJoin`, at the landing edge): its cells in the hash, its collider teleported to its pose and on, its authored `invulnerable` back. It is a building of the base like any other: the units bite it, Nav paths around it, the carriers reach it. |
| Placement | The aim meets the DECK (`aimGroundPoint`: the ray against the plane of the deck top over its tiles, when that is nearer than the terrain hit): the ghost is the footprint on the deck's tiles, turned and tilted with the ship (`drawShipGhost`), green when `deckCellsFree` (every tile on the deck, not the pilot's tile, none of another ship structure's - except a CANNON on a RAIL tile: it shares the rail's tile) and nobody stands there. A rotated placement (the Cannon) keeps its own turn on the deck (`Ref::shipTurn`, eighths relative to the ship: `shipStructureRot`, saved as `ShipTurn`). **A barracks on the deck makes no unit while the ship is not landed** (`NpcSystem::service` refunds its spawn like a failed one: the build bar stays full, the unit comes out once the ship has landed). A click queues `queueShipPlaceRequest` (authority only); `placeShipStructure` checks again and spawns (`spawnShipStructure`). Every placeable type but the road pieces (`isShipPlaceableType`): the deck's free tiles are road. A wall or a gate is one piece (no drag line on the deck). A Lumberyard / Quarry surveys the world under its tile. Flying or landed alike. While the ship is landed a LAND placement on a deck cell is refused (`footprintAreaClear`). |
| THE DECK IS ROAD | Landed, a free deck tile is a road cell (`roadAt`: no occupant in the hash and `worldCellOnDeck`) at the deck top, so every building on the deck is on the road, and a road built up to an ENTRANCE joins the deck: the workers walk on and off. Flying there is no road on the deck (the walkers on it go to the nearest road cell or home, as on any road that is gone). A built RAIL tile is road too (at the deck top), and FOR THE GUARDS AND THE MAGES it is a WALKWAY DECK (`guardDeckAt`): their posts take rail tiles first, and from there they need no clear line to a target (they shoot over the rail and the walls). A free deck tile is plain road. |
| THE RAIL | The ship's wall: a `ShipRail` structure on every EDGE tile (`GameShip::railSides`: the sides whose neighbour is off the deck), but not on the ENTRANCE tiles (`GameShip::entrances`, the gaps in the wall: two tiles on each side amidships, z 4 .. 8; one on each side forward, z -16 .. -14; one in the middle of the stern). `ensureShipRails` also removes a rail that stands on an entrance tile (an older save). `shipRail.pre`: the tile's sill plate (the root's mesh - a structure needs one, the units find what to bite by it) and four rail pieces along the tile's edges (`Models/shipRail.obj`, 1 m tall, 0.4 m thick), enabled on the open sides by `applyRailSides` (again after rubble is rebuilt). THE TILE IS WALKED ON: the prefab has no collider; `buildRailBody` makes a static body (the entity as its user data, contact events on) of only the rail pieces on the open sides (`railPieces`), so a body walks on the tile but not off the deck - landed and not rubble only; aloft the ship's body carries the same pieces (`shipColliderBoxes`). PATHING (`gatherNavFeed`): a built rail is OPEN for its own team (like a gate: the own army paths along the rail tiles; the enemy breaches it), and its rail pieces are Nav EDGE WALLS (`NavObstacle::edges`, the open sides in world axes: `railWorldSides`) for its own team and the raster and the goals - so the own army walks the rail but never paths off the ship across it, only through an entrance; no cell outside is blocked (a road may run there). The enemy's field has no edge walls: it comes up to the rail from outside and bites it. The walkers' road search does the same (`railEdgeBetween` in `roadSearch`, the guard posts' search and `touchesRoad`: no step across a rail piece). A FLYING ship's structures are no Nav obstacle or target at all (`gatherNavFeed` skips them: they are in the air). THE COST: the deck and rail checks of `roadAt`, `railEdgeBetween` and `guardDeckAt` first test `nearDeck` - the landed deck's world cell rectangle (one cell of margin, `m_deckCellMin` / `m_deckCellMax`, set at the landing edge) - so a cell away from the ship costs two compares, no hash lookup or pose math. Health "Ship rail health" (600), rubble and repair like a Wall ("Ship rail repair cost"), breachable for Nav. Never placed (`isPlaceableType`), but stone builds and repairs it (`isRepairableType`: the Builder's hut, the walkers' build and repair work, the players); a body beside or on its rubble does not block the rebuild (`actorInFootprint` skips it: the tile is walked on); never deleted; `ensureShipRails` spawns the missing ones (the start, after a load). |
| THE BEACON | On the deck at the tile (0, -1) (`GameShip::baseCell`: 3 x 3, on the middle line; tried a 10-tile deck, dropped because the Beacon stood 1 m off the middle), `spawnShipBase`: the start minerals and a full store, as `spawnBase`. While landed its ground is `m_basePos` (the waves' home, the fields' bake slice 1 m over it in `tickBeacon`); flying, `m_basePos` stays where it last landed. **Flying by day it has no bubble** (`tickProduction`: the field's target output is 0 while the ship is not `Landed` and `fieldNight` < 0.5); the bubble ramps back at dusk or once the ship has landed. |
| THE WAVES | `waveTargetFrom`: a wave marches to the NEAREST own-team building from its origin that can be attacked (not invulnerable, not rubble, no road piece), 6 m short of it on the wave's side. A flying ship's are invulnerable, so the ship is never the target in the air; a landed ship's are. No such building: the ground under the ship. |
| Sync | None: the ship's structures are the authority's. No GPl is sent for them (`sendStructurePlaced` skips them), so a client sees neither the ship nor what stands on it. |

## The player on board

* **On the deck** (`GameShip::onDeck`: over the deck and up to 3.5 m over it): `GamePlayer::setPlatform` gives the deck's
  velocity at the player (`pointVelocity`: the spin included). `tickMovement` walks RELATIVE to it (the wanted velocity
  is the deck's plus the walk, at 3x the accel) and holds the vertical velocity to the deck's while standing; a jump
  adds to it. It runs with no input too (window unfocused), so the ship never leaves a player behind.
  **The hold is for STANDING only**: with the ground ray (`footGap`, 0.5 m) finding nothing under the feet, the player
  falls freely over the deck (tried the hold whenever over the deck, dropped because a jump or a fall onto a landed
  deck then sank slowly).
* **THE HELM: F** (a tap: F held for a second clears the RALLY FLAG instead, and an F tap anywhere else sets it - see
  Hotbar.md) with the crosshair ON THE BEACON (`onFTap`: `hoveredStructure` is the ship's Base) takes it, from anywhere -
  the deck, the ground, under a flying hull (the aim meets the deck from below): the player is teleported to
  `helmLocal()` (the tile right behind the Beacon, x 1 z 3; no wheel is drawn) when it is not there, so BOARDING a
  flying ship is the same tap. `m_helmGrace` (0.5 s) holds the "too far from the helm" check while the queued teleport
  lands. The HUD counter "Ship" says "F: pilot" while the crosshair is on the Beacon (the labels' hover). F again lets
  go; to get off, walk or jump over the rail (a landed ship: through an entrance, or down its stairs).
  **At the helm**: WASD and Space / Ctrl are the ship's (`setControlsLocked`: the player's walk, jump and sprint are off), X brakes, L places a landing / takes off - and so does every other BUILD KEY (Tab, Q, E: there is no
  building at the helm; while the landing ghost is up, Q / E turn it), the build keys are off and an open build UI closes, the player is held at its place (a pull toward it on
  top of the deck's velocity), and the camera orbits the SHIP's middle at "Camera distance (m)" (55, at least;
  `GameCamera::applyThirdPerson`'s `minDistance`). More than 8 m from the place lets go by itself. The counter is SHORT
  ("fly 60% 12m/s 23m" / "sail 60% 8m/s" / "landing" / "landed  L: lift", or for 3 s why it cannot land: the counter
  column is narrow, and a long line ran over its own label).

## Known gaps

* The authority only: a client sees no ship and nothing on it (no sync yet), and a client's player cannot board it or
  build on it.
* It passes through trees, rocks and buildings in flight (kinematic: no collision with static bodies); only its height
  keeps it over them, and the height follows the terrain alone.
* Nav does not know it while it flies: units under it are pushed away, units on it do not path on it.
* A unit or a player under the deck when it lands is pushed by the hull.
* No cargo, no combat yet.
